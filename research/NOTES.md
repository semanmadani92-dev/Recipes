# Research notes: world markets and store prices

Collected 2026-11-17 by the researcher agent. **Do not edit the JSON by hand.** It is generated by `research/_build/regen_all.py`, which runs `gen_prices.py`, then `gen_markets.py`, then the level check. Put new data in `_build/markets_data.py` or `_build/prices_data.py` and run `python3 research/_build/regen_all.py`.

## What is here

| File | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `markets.json` | 156 countries: the 154 recipe countries plus Niger (NE) and Chad (TD), the other countries over 20 M people. Each has the currency, the rate per EUR, the grocery price level against DE, shelf factors, stores, sources and a confidence rating. |
| `prices/<CC>.json` | Real observed prices (shelf prices or published averages), one row per observation. The current counts are in the summary at the end of this file. |

Confidence in `markets.json`:
- **high**: the 37 markets already in `js/data/markets.js`, with rates updated to Nov 2026.
- **medium**: large or well-documented markets.
- **low**: small markets, war or crisis economies, and countries where the currency is not stable.

## How the data was collected (important)

In this session the web was **search-only**. WebFetch and curl to all external hosts were blocked by the egress proxy (numbeo.com, ecb.europa.eu, bancaditalia.it, open.er-api.com and others). Every price row therefore comes from text I saw in **search-result snippets and summaries** of the cited page. The URL in each row is the page the figure was attributed to. I did not invent any prices. Some attributions are approximate, though: a few rows cite two URLs, and the Yangon row is marked "URL as shown in search results". To refresh the data, allow numbeo.com / bls.gov / ons.gov.uk / the retailer sites and re-scrape.

## Price level (`level`, DE = 1)

- **Starting point:** World Bank ICP 2021 food PLIs, Eurostat PLI 2024, and the existing levels in markets.js. For Europe I cross-checked against the Numbeo groceries index as republished by Euronews (2026-10-24, EU = 100): DE 111.6, IT 110.2, ES 89.7, CH 189.2, RO 66.7, MD 57.2, MK 59.8, BA 67.9, RS 68.8.
- **Correction against observed prices:** each country with price rows gets a `level_check` object. It holds the median of (observed price / modelled estimate), normalised so DE = 1.
- **Which levels were changed:** where the deviation was above 17 %, the country had at least 4 rows, and confidence was not "high", I moved the level halfway in log terms (multiplied it by the square root of the ratio). The original value is kept in `adjusted_from`.
- **Changed this way (35 countries):** EE SK HR UA RU UZ KG KW JO IQ IR IL MA TN NG SN ET KE NA BW TW PH SG MM GT HN SV NI DO JM AR CO VE EC UY. Treat these corrections as rough.
- **Signals I did not apply to high-confidence markets:** US 0.70, GB 0.73, CA 0.74, PK 0.68 and BD 0.57 (these suggest the levels are too high), and BR, CN and ID about 0.77. The samples are mostly milk, eggs, rice and chicken, and US eggs were unusually cheap in 2026. Builder: use these as a hint, not as truth.

## Stores

- **The factor is relative to the main mainstream discounter or hypermarket (= 1).** As in the existing markets.js, fresh markets and bulk stores can go below 1.
- **Generic names** are only "Fresh market", "Weekly market", "Corner shop" and "Local grocers".
- **Generic labels changed in the existing markets:**
  - Changed to "Fresh market": the old Mercado, Mercato, Rynok, Local bazaar, Wet market, Chợ, Pasar, Souq, Open market, Open-air market and Traditional market.
  - BR "Feira" became "Weekly market".
  - Still to translate or keep (left as they were): "Kirana · sabzi mandi", "Kacha bazar", "Mercado / tianguis", "Turkish & Asian grocers", "Asian & world food grocers" and "Asian & Latino grocers".
- **Every store id from `js/data/markets.js` is kept unchanged** (checked by script), so saved user settings stay valid. New stores were added with new ids, for example `in_bigbasket`, `ar_chino` and the stores of the 119 new countries.

## Prices: format notes for the importer

- **`kind`:** `shelf` means a single store; `average` means statistics office data or a Numbeo-style average; `list` means an MRP or list price.
- **Units** are g, ml or pc. Cans count as pc, with the can size in `product` or `note`.
- **Items sold by weight whose German unit is pieces:** bananas, lemon, orange, lettuce, eggs (ID and CN quote eggs per kg), garlic (German unit is clove) and avocado. These rows have `unit: "g"` plus `pc_weight_g` (banana 120, lemon 110, lime 67, orange 200, lettuce head 570, egg 60, garlic clove 5, avocado 200). Convert these, or skip them.
- **USD-quoted averages** (BearSavings, CityCost, openvan, whereandwhen) were converted to local currency at the stated rate. The conversion is noted in `product` or `source`.
- **Ranges were reduced to their midpoint.** The original range is in `product`.
- **The `date` field** is "YYYY-MM", "YYYY-MM-DD", "2026", "undated" or "2026 (undated listing)". Weight older rows lower:
  - TR Istanbul rows are from Nov 2025, and Turkish food inflation is about 34 %/yr.
  - RU Moscow trek.zone rows are from July 2025.
  - TW expatarrivals rows are from Nov 2025.
- **Product mismatches to watch:**
  - `cheddar` rows are often "local cheese" (noted).
  - Some `chicken_breast` rows are whole chicken (noted).
  - `pork_shoulder` US/CN rows are chops or lean pork (noted).
  - `beef_stew` EG/ID rows are generic "beef meat".

## Countries needing care (currency or data)

- **AR:** inflation is still high (food basket +28 % year to date). The peso floats around 1,700/EUR. Refresh monthly.
- **VE:** the BCV official rate is 1,009/EUR and rising about 1 %/week. Shops often price in USD, and the parallel rate is about 15 % above official. Low confidence.
- **IR:** the open-market rial was about 2.0-2.3 M/USD in 2026 after a war shock, and inflation is the highest since WWII. Prices are very unstable, so treat as illustrative.
- **NG:** food inflation was 20 % in July 2026. The naira is stable near 1,500/EUR.
- **SY:** new pound (two zeros removed). The official rate is 122.5/USD and the parallel rate about 150.
- **LB:** the LBP rate is 89,500/USD and many prices are in USD.
- **Several others:**
  - **SD:** war economy.
  - **YE:** split currency.
  - **MM:** official vs market rate.
  - **CU:** official vs informal rate.
  - **MW and DZ:** parallel markets.
  - **TM:** the official peg is far from the street rate.
  - **KP:** no data; the entry is a placeholder.
  - **ZW:** priced in USD.
  - **BO:** currency stress.
  - **ET:** floated in 2024.
